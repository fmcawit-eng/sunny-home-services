Signs Your Furnace Needs Replacement

A handful of signs tell you that your furnace might be near its end. West Jordan’s long, high-altitude winters tend to bring the end faster. Most units last 15 to 20 years, and since the typical local home dates to the mid-1990s, there are many original furnaces right in that danger zone. Here’s a quick list of checks you can do yourself for a possible furnace replacement. If you’re nodding to three or more, it’s worth an assessment:

  • ✓ Repeated, pricey repairs for the last couple of Wasatch winters
  • ✓ A rising gas bill when your heating habits haven’t changed
  • ✓ Rooms that don’t warm up evenly
  • ✓ A burner flame that's yellow or flickering instead of a clean blue flame
  • ✓ Strange noises like banging, rattling, or a startup boom
  • ✓ A furnace that's 15 years or older

Benefits of Upgrading to an Energy-Efficient Furnace in Your Wasatch Front Home

A new energy-efficient furnace starts paying for itself quickly. A furnace’s AFUE is its annual fuel utilization efficiency. That’s basically the ‘miles-per-gallon’ of your furnace. Many older units get by at 70% AFUE or lower. The U.S. Department of Energy explains that a 90% AFUE furnace converts 90% of its fuel into heat, with the rest lost. A modern condensing furnace turns up to 98% of its fuel into heat.

A furnace can only deliver its rated efficiency if it’s sized for where it’s running, and that’s where the big chains miss our local reality. West Jordan sits at around 4,300 feet, and a furnace has to be derated for that altitude to fire efficiently. If a furnace here is sized for sea-level conditions, it’ll lose capacity and burn extra fuel before our first cold snap. Modern units also run quieter, heat more evenly, and add a layer of safety with sealed combustion. Swap out an aging furnace, and many West Jordan homeowners trim up to 40% off their winter gas bill.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I install a gas or electric furnace?
For most West Jordan homes with a gas line, a gas furnace is the cheaper option during our cold winters. Electric furnaces make sense for homes without gas service or smaller spaces. We’ll weigh your fuel costs and layout during the free assessment and recommend the right fit.
Do I need a permit for furnace installation in West Jordan?
Yes, a permit is required for furnace installation in West Jordan, and a licensed contractor like us pulls it for you. The West Jordan Building and Safety Division reviews the work to ensure it meets the Utah Mechanical Code. We handle the paperwork and inspection.
Are there tax credits for furnace installation in Utah?
There's no federal tax credit for furnace installation in 2026. The credit that covered high-efficiency furnaces expired at the end of 2025. You may still qualify for a natural gas rebate through your utility's ThermWise program, and we'll point you to whatever's available when we quote your system.
What AFUE rating should I get?
For a cold, high-altitude climate like ours, aim for a furnace with an AFUE of 90% or higher. The minimum for new furnaces is 80% AFUE, and condensing models reach up to 98%. A higher rating costs more upfront but burns less gas every winter.
