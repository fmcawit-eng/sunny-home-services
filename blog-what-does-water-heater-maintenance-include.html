What Do Plumbers Check During Water Heater Maintenance?

Book Now All Articles

During water heater maintenance, plumbers fully inspect the tank, safety features, and performance to catch problems while they’re still small. They check the tank, anode rod, safety valve, burner or heating elements, and venting, then flush out the sediment that Utah’s hard water leaves behind. If you’re curious what actually happens during a tune-up and whether it’s worth scheduling one, this guide walks you through the complete water heater maintenance checklist. Learn what’s included in tank and tankless service, the warning signs to watch for, and why Wasatch Front homes need service more often than most.

Key Takeaways

  • Water heater maintenance includes flushing the tank, inspecting the pressure relief valve and the anode rod, checking the burner or elements, and confirming the temperature and venting.
  • Utah’s hard water causes sediment and scale to build up faster, making annual service essential for most homes.
  • Tank heaters need annual service, while tankless units often require two descaling visits per year.

What Does Preventative Maintenance on a Hot Water Heater Include?

Preventative maintenance on a hot water heater includes a complete visual inspection, a full tank flush, safety valve inspection, and checks on the components. The water heater inspection checklist starts on the outside, covering the tank condition, fittings, supply lines, and the surrounding floor. Next, the inspector checks the temperature and pressure relief valve, which protects the tank from dangerous pressure buildup. Plumbers then drain and flush the tank to remove sediment, check the anode rod, and inspect the burner assembly on gas units or the heating elements on electric ones.

Signs your water heater needs maintenance

If it’s been over a year since your last service, or you’ve noticed any of the following symptoms, it’s time to schedule water heater maintenance:

  • Rumbling, popping, or crackling from the tank
  • Hot water running out faster than it used to
  • Rusty or cloudy water from the hot tap
  • A rotten-egg smell in the hot water only
  • Moisture, drips, or corrosion around the base of the tank
  • Rising gas or electric bills even though your usage is the same

Notice a rumbling sound coming from your water heater? That’s water boiling underneath a layer of sediment. It’s especially common in Wasatch Front homes without a water softener. Sediment buildup can reduce efficiency, raise your water bills, and overheat the bottom of the tank.

Common water heater issues found during a maintenance visit

The most common water heater issues found during maintenance are sediment buildup, worn anode rods, faulty T&P valves, and thermostat or venting problems. Most issues just require a quick fix and are inexpensive to repair when they’re caught early. Sediment is the main problem found in Utah homes. Dissolved minerals settle at the bottom of the tank, insulate the water from the burner, and force the unit to run longer than necessary to reach the set temperature. Anode rod wear is another frequent finding. The anode is a sacrificial metal rod that corrodes so the tank doesn’t. Once it wears away completely, rust starts to form on the tank itself.

Tank Water Heater Maintenance Checklist

Tank water heater maintenance covers the tank, the safety components, the heating system, and water quality. During your service, a plumber will:

  • Inspect the tank, fittings, and surrounding area for leaks or corrosion.
  • Inspect the T&P relief valve and make sure the discharge pipe is properly routed.
  • Drain and flush the tank to remove sediment.
  • Pull the anode rod and replace it if it’s heavily corroded.
  • Check the burner, pilot, and thermocouple on gas units, or test the elements on electric units.
  • Verify the thermostat and adjust it if needed.
  • Inspect the flue and draft hood for proper venting.
  • Check the expansion tank and confirm its pressure.
  • Test the shutoff valve and inspect the supply lines.

Tankless Water Heater Maintenance Checklist

Tankless water heater maintenance focuses on descaling the heat exchanger, the main cause of tankless failures in hard-water areas like the Wasatch Front. Tankless units can last for more than 20 years, but only if mineral buildup is routinely cleared. A full tankless maintenance service includes:

  • Isolating the unit and circulating a descaling solution through the heat exchanger for 45 to 60 minutes.
  • Cleaning or replacing the inlet water filter screen.
  • Inspecting and cleaning the burner and combustion chamber.
  • Checking the venting and air intake for blockages.
  • Verifying gas pressure and electrical connections.
  • Inspecting condensate lines on condensing models.
  • Reviewing error codes and diagnostic history.
  • Confirming the output temperature matches the setpoint.

Best Water Heater Maintenance Schedule We Recommend

The best hot water heater maintenance schedule (tank and tankless heaters) for Wasatch Front homes is once a year. Sunny Home Services recommends a schedule that works for most Utah homes:

  • Every year: Book a full professional maintenance visit, including a flush or descaling, T&P, and anode inspection.
  • Every three to five years: Replace the anode rod before the tank starts corroding.
  • After eight years: Start budgeting for water heater replacement if you have a tank system and watch for warning signs that the tank is nearing the end of its lifespan.
  • After 15 years: Explore your replacement options for tankless systems and compare the cost of water heater repairs against a new, more efficient unit.

Importance of Water Heater Maintenance in Wasatch Front Homes

The Wasatch Front’s hard water, cold winters, and basement installations make water heater maintenance more important here than in other areas. The mineral-heavy water that runs through Ogden to Provo leaves scale in tanks and heat exchangers that builds up every year. When water temperatures drop into the 40s in mid-November, your water heater has to work harder to heat every gallon. If sediment is coating the inside of the tank, your system runs longer, costs more to operate, and is more likely to fail. Many homes in Davis and Weber County also keep the water heater in finished basements. If your tank fails and leaks, water damage can add thousands to your replacement bill and disrupt your household for weeks. Scheduling an annual checkup is the best insurance to avoid that scenario.
