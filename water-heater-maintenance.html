Problems Regular Maintenance Prevents

Water heater maintenance along the Wasatch Front is really about preventing the sediment problems our hard water causes before they turn into repairs or a full replacement.

Utah’s mineral-heavy water is the main culprit: it drops calcium and lime to the bottom of the tank, coats the elements, and wears out the anode rod. Skip maintenance for a few years and those small issues compound into a struggling, noisy, inefficient heater.

Routine service helps you avoid:

  • Lost capacity as sediment takes up space meant for hot water.
  • Rising energy bills from a burner working through a layer of scale.
  • Rumbling and popping as hardened sediment cooks on the tank floor.
  • Early tank corrosion once the anode rod is used up.
  • Rusty water and premature tank failure.
  • Surprise breakdowns during cold-weather peak demand.

Along the Wasatch Front specifically, maintenance pays off in both newer and older homes:

  • In newer homes, annual flushing keeps builder-grade tanks from filling with sediment in their first few years.
  • In older homes across the valley, regular service helps aging heaters reach the end of their expected life instead of failing early.

Across the Salt Lake Valley, sediment is the number one reason water heaters wear out before their time. A yearly flush is the simplest, most cost-effective way to protect the unit.

Signs Your Water Heater Is Overdue for Maintenance

Knowing when your water heater along the Wasatch Front needs service, versus when it may be time to plan a replacement, comes down to how the unit is behaving and how our hard water has treated it.

Along the Wasatch Front homes, most heaters benefit from a flush every year given our mineral-heavy water. If your unit is showing warning signs but is otherwise sound, maintenance can bring it back. If those signs come with real age and rust, it may be time to plan ahead.

Schedule maintenance when:
  • It’s been more than a year since your last flush.
  • You hear rumbling or popping from the tank.
  • Hot water runs out faster than it used to.
  • Energy bills are creeping up without a clear cause.
  • You’ve never had the anode rod checked.
It may be time to plan a replacement when:
  • The tank is 10 to 15+ years old.
  • Water comes out rusty or smells metallic.
  • The tank body is leaking, not just a fitting.
  • Even after a flush, capacity stays low.
