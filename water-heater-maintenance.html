What’s Included in a Water Heater Tune-Up

Even the most perfectly installed water heater needs occasional maintenance and inspections to work at its best, and that’s particularly true here in the Wasatch Front. With such a high mineral content in the municipal water supply, it’s common for water heaters to slow down as sediment builds, leading to higher energy bills, inconsistent water heating, and a greater risk of breakdown. Here’s what you can expect when you work with our team for a water heater tune-up:

1
We begin with a multi-point inspection of your system, checking for areas of rust on the tank, dampness in the surrounding area, and any other signs of a leak, such as a musty odor or visible mold growth.
2
We conduct safety checks, such as inspecting the temperature and pressure (T&P) relief valve to make sure it’s functioning correctly.
3
For gas-powered units, we inspect the gas line and confirm that the burner and pilot light assembly are functioning properly.
4
For electric units, we’ll test the heating elements to ensure they have the correct resistance.
5
We flush the water heater tank to remove any mineral sediment or rust buildup that may be compromising water quality.
6
If you have a tank-style water heater, we’ll check the sacrificial anode rod and, if needed, replace it to maintain protection against corrosion.
7
With the unit cleaned and inspected, we recalibrate the temperature settings to confirm that the system meets safe output temperature settings, typically between 120°F and 125°F.
8
We put everything back together, run a few final tests, clean up the work area, and provide you with our recommendations for future water heater repairs or ongoing maintenance.

Why Water Heater Preventive Maintenance Matters in West Jordan

As any West Jordan homeowner knows, water in the Wasatch Front is notorious for its very high mineral content. This is what causes those troublesome water spots on faucets and dishes and chalky white buildup wherever you use water regularly. What you may not realize is that this mineral content also has a big impact on your water heater.

In tank-style systems, mineral buildup forms sediment at the bottom, which can accumulate and essentially cook over time. This has major effects on your system’s performance and efficiency: the more sediment in a tank, the more energy the heater needs to do its job. Plus, high levels of sediment can cause noisy rumbling or popping during operation and put your unit at risk of a serious breakdown in the future.

By keeping up with ongoing preventative maintenance on a hot water heater, you’ll reduce monthly energy bills, enjoy a more consistent supply of hot water, and significantly reduce the chances of a leak, water heater shutdown, or even a dangerous pressure explosion.

Water heater maintenance isn’t just for tank-style units, either. Tankless water heaters may lack a sediment reservoir, but minerals in West Jordan water can still accumulate on the heat exchanger over time. This will dramatically reduce system efficiency and performance and can even reduce the system’s overall lifespan.

How Often Should You Flush or Service Your Water Heater

For the best results, we suggest getting water heater tank maintenance once a year. Sediment can build up faster than you may realize in West Jordan, and flushing a tank proactively is always easier (and cheaper) than waiting for the problem to reach a critical point. You can also flush your tank on your own, though you’ll need an annual visit from a plumbing professional to conduct other safety checks and component tune-ups.

Frequently Asked Questions

How often should I flush my water heater in West Jordan?
For the best results, we recommend flushing your water heater once or twice a year in West Jordan. In this area, the water has a very high mineral content, making regular flushing an essential maintenance task.
What happens if I never maintain my water heater?
If you never maintain your water heater, you risk high energy bills, system failures, or even a catastrophic pressure explosion. Not to mention, you’ll constantly run out of hot water in your home.
What are the signs of a failing water heater?
Signs of a failing water heater include rusty or discolored water, loud noises from the system, leaks, and severe fluctuations in water temperature. You’ll also know your system will need replacement soon if it’s more than 10 to 12 years old.
