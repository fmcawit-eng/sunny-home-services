Common Reasons Wasatch Front Homes Need a New Sewer Line

Sewer line installation along the Wasatch Front usually comes down to a few situations driven by the age of the pipe, local soil, and property changes.

Utah’s heavy clay soils and mature landscaping are hard on older lines. Other local factors add up too: decades-old clay and cast iron pipe reaches end of life, ground movement finally breaks a failing line, and new construction requires a fresh connection to the main.

Sunny Home Services’ plumbers frequently install new sewer lines when:

  • An old clay or cast iron line has failed beyond repair.
  • Roots and cracks have compromised the entire run.
  • A line has collapsed or bellied in multiple places.
  • A room addition or ADU needs a new connection.
  • A new build requires a full sewer lateral.
  • Repeated backups make repair no longer cost-effective.

Along the Wasatch Front specifically, we handle installations across different property types:

  • In older neighborhoods, replacing brittle clay pipe with modern PVC extends the system’s life for decades.
  • In newer developments and additions, we run correctly graded lines built for the local soil.

Across the Salt Lake Valley, a camera inspection and proper grading plan are the foundation of every installation, so the new line drains right from day one.

When to Repair vs Install a New Sewer Line

Knowing whether to repair your existing Wasatch Front sewer line or install a new one comes down to the condition of the pipe, how many sections are failing, and whether the line is at the end of its usable life.

An isolated problem in otherwise sound pipe is a repair. When the line is aging clay or cast iron failing in multiple spots, a full installation gives you a modern, root-resistant line and ends the cycle of repeat repairs.

A repair may be enough when:
  • Damage is limited to one section of the line.
  • The rest of the pipe is in good condition.
  • The line is relatively modern PVC or ABS.
  • A single root intrusion or crack is the issue.
A new installation makes sense when:
  • The line is aging clay or cast iron near end of life.
  • The camera shows damage along multiple sections.
  • Backups keep returning after prior repairs.
  • You’re adding a room, ADU, or new build.
  • You want a modern, root-resistant line for decades of service.
