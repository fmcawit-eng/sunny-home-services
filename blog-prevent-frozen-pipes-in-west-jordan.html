How to Prevent Frozen Pipes Before the First Freeze in West Jordan

Book Now All Articles

Preventing frozen drain pipes in your West Jordan home means taking precautions like ensuring you have proper insulation, keeping an eye on temperature forecasts, and keeping your home at a consistent temperature, day and night, during a cold snap. West Jordan winters can be brutally cold, with frequent snowfalls and freezing nighttime temperatures, so when you have frozen drain pipes, knowing what to do can prevent serious flooding and costly repairs. Let’s explore prevention methods, as well as what to do (and what not to do) when your plumbing system ices up.

When Do Pipes Start Freezing in West Jordan?

During West Jordan winters, temperatures frequently drop below freezing. Your pipes can start to freeze as soon as the surrounding air temperature reaches 32°F or lower, especially if they’re not insulated, but the serious risk of freezing develops after temperatures drop below 20°F. Areas of the house most likely to freeze are those nearest the exterior walls of the house, or those with less insulation or access to heated air, like crawl spaces or attached garages.

How to Prevent Frozen Pipes From Bursting

Wondering how to prevent frozen pipes from bursting? The best way to avoid burst pipes is to stop them from freezing in the first place. Some steps you can take to avoid frozen pipes in the winter include:

  • Add insulation: Properly insulated plumbing won’t freeze in the winter. Pay attention to vulnerable spaces like attics, garages, crawl spaces, and basements.
  • Apply heat tape or pipe sleeves: These products are designed to keep heat in to reduce the risk of burst pipes.
  • Let water drip: Turn the faucets on to a slow trickle, so the moving water does not have a chance to freeze in the pipes.
  • Keep your home temperature steady: Don’t let cold winter nights drop the temperature inside your home as you sleep.
  • Seal air leaks around the house: Stop drafts from getting in under doors, dryer vents, and gaps around windows.

If pipes in your house have slow water flow, pipes start bulging, you can see ice or frost on exposed pipes, or your plumbing feels cold to the touch, call a Sunny Home Services plumber for help. We’re fast and dependable, and can help thaw your pipes and check for damage.

Pre-Winter Freeze Plumbing Checklist

So, how do you prevent frozen drain pipes? There are a few key ways to protect your home before the winter chill arrives:

  • Disconnect the hoses and turn off water to outdoor hose bibs.
  • Cover your outdoor faucets.
  • Drain the sprinkler system.
  • Seal air gaps around the house.
  • Install heat tape and insulation where needed.

If you plan on going away for more than a day, set your home temperature to a minimum of 50°F, open up cabinet doors to allow warm air to circulate properly, and let your faucets drip slightly.

What to Do if You Have Frozen Drain Pipes

If your drain pipe is frozen, stop using the affected fixtures, open the drain or tap to relieve pressure, and give water a place to escape as the ice melts. You can then apply hot towels, heating pads, or heat from a hair dryer to gently warm up the affected pipes. If your sewer drains are frozen, stop using all of your indoor plumbing immediately, including the toilets, appliances, sinks, and showers, as adding water to the frozen drain will cause sewage backup into your home.

What Not to Do:

If you suspect your drain pipes are frozen, there are a few things you should never do. Avoid:

  • Pouring boiling water down the drain, as the extreme temperature difference can cause pipe materials to crack and warp.
  • Using a torch, propane heater, or open flames to try and melt the ice. Not only can this damage or melt pipes, but it can also become a fire hazard.
  • Using force, including hitting the outside of the pipe, or pushing a snake down a frozen line. Doing so would risk rupturing the brittle pipe material.

What to Do If a Pipe Bursts

A burst pipe is a plumbing emergency. Call us for plumbing repair. While you wait for your plumber to arrive, you can turn the water off, shut off your water heater, open up the faucets to relieve pressure, and, if safe to do so, shut down the power to areas with pooling or dripping water to prevent electric shock. Frequently Asked Questions

How do I know if a drain is frozen or clogged?
While both a frozen drain and a clogged drain prevent normal water flow, a frozen drain will feel cold to the touch and may make sounds like popping or clanking as the ice expands in the pipe. Clogged drains may sound more like gurgling; they might smell bad, and are usually localized to a single fixture.
How do I prevent frozen pipes from bursting?
To prevent frozen pipes from bursting, open up the taps to help relieve pressure, and then apply gentle heat to the outside of the pipe, such as a heating pad, a towel soaked in hot water, or a hair dryer.
Can I pour hot water down a frozen drain?
Do not pour boiling water down a frozen drain. The thermal shock can crack pipes and weaken joints. It may also melt plastic pipes that aren’t designed to handle boiling temperatures. Small amounts of warm or hot water are okay to pour slowly down the drain.
