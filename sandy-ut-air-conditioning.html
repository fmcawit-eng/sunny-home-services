Signs your AC needs repair

These are signs that indicate your AC is in need of repair:

  • Air from the vents is weak or not cold.
  • The system short cycles, turning on and off every few minutes.
  • There’s a grinding, buzzing, or rattling sound from the outdoor unit.
  • You find ice on the refrigerant line or evaporator coil.
  • Your cooling bill’s jumped without a change in usage.
  • Rooms in the house never reach temperature.

New central air conditioner installation

A properly sized central system delivers even temperatures and predictable bills. Our AC installation work includes verifying airflow, and checking refrigerant charge against manufacturer specifications.

Ductless mini split installation

A ductless mini split is usually the most practical answer for finished basements, additions, garages, upstairs bedrooms, and bonus rooms with no ductwork. If you’re weighing efficiency and year-round comfort, ask us about heat pumps as well.

Swamp cooler to central AC conversion

We can convert your system from a swamp cooler to central air to provide you with consistent cooling, filtration, and a thermostat that works, all of which swamp coolers struggle with. These conversions may require new ductwork or duct modifications, which we’ll assess before quoting.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I know if I need a repair or a full AC replacement?
Deciding between a repair and replacement starts with comparing the repair cost to the system’s age. A minor repair on an 8-year-old unit is worth doing. A major repair on a 14-year-old unit probably isn’t, particularly if it runs R-22 refrigerant or has failed more than once recently.
How long does AC installation take?
A straightforward central AC replacement typically takes one day. Swamp cooler conversions, duct modifications, and mini-split installations can take two to three days depending on scope.
How long should an AC unit last in Sandy?
Most central air conditioners last 12 to 15 years here. Sandy’s long summer run times and wide daily temperature swings work systems harder than milder climates, so units that skip annual maintenance often fall short of that.
