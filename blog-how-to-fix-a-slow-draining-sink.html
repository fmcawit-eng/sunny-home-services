How to Fix a Slow Draining Sink

Book Now All Articles

Whether you’re in Bountiful, Spanish Fork, or another part of the Wasatch Front, much of the water supply in our region is several times harder than the national average. While having hard water is one cause of a slow drain, this common problem may occur for myriad reasons. If you’re wondering how to fix a slow draining sink and when you should call a plumber for help, the pros at Sunny Home Services have some insightful information to help you deal with this frustrating issue.

How to Fix a Slow Draining Sink: Step by Step

Returning home to a slow-draining sink after a beautiful day bird-watching and exploring Antelope Island State Park is certainly an unpleasant experience. Fortunately, there are some things you can do yourself to address a slow draining sink before having to call a plumber for professional drain cleaning services. Follow these steps to see if you can remedy the issue on your own before contacting Sunny Home Services for help.

Clean the sink stopper

Soap scum, grease, hair, and other debris can easily become an issue for your drains over time. Start by carefully removing the sink stopper at your bathroom or kitchen sink, and look for signs of blockages or buildup. If you see anything under the stopper, carefully remove it using your hands (wear gloves to stay clean), and dispose of it in the trash. Once you’ve removed the buildup, you can place the stopper back into the drain and see if that resolved the issue.

Try a plunger

Using a standard household plunger often helps to dislodge small amounts of debris from bathroom and kitchen sinks. Start by removing the stopper or sink hole cover, then add enough water so it covers the bottom of the plunger by about one inch. Center the rubber cup directly over the drain opening, and pump the plunger up and down for approximately 15 seconds before pulling it straight up and out of the sink.

Clean out your P-trap

The P-trap is the curved section of pipe underneath your sink, and it can easily get clogged over time. Place a pan or small bucket right underneath this area to catch gunk and water before loosening the slip-nut connectors on both ends of the P-trap (these should be easy to remove by hand). Gently remove the P-trap and throw away any trapped debris that comes out into the garbage. Rinse the trap in a different sink before putting it back in place and tightening the nuts. After this step, make sure you fill your sink with water and release the stopper to confirm that there are no leaks.

Use a drain snake

If your drains are still slow after following the previous steps, you may need to use a drain snake to reach clogs lodged deeper inside the pipes. Feed the flexible snake down into the pipe opening and rotate the handle clockwise, pushing gently until you feel resistance. Twist the drain snake to break up the blockage and then slowly pull it out. After throwing away the debris, flush your pipe with hot water to remove any remaining gunk.

Causes of a Bathroom Sink Draining Slowly

A bathroom sink draining slowly may be caused by:

  • Strands of hair or soap scum
  • Limescale or calcium buildup from hard water
  • Small objects like cotton swabs or hair ties
  • Leaves or debris blocking roof vent pipes

Causes of a Kitchen Sink Draining Slowly

Here are some reasons why your kitchen sink may be draining slowly:

  • An accumulation of food particles or coffee grounds
  • Garbage disposal likely has too much food or other debris built up inside the P-trap
  • Narrowed pipes from fats, oils, and grease (FOGs)

What Not to Do if Your Sink Is Draining Slowly

There are some common drain and garbage disposal problems and fixes in West Jordan homes that homeowners can try, but it’s also important to understand what not to do if your sink is draining slowly:

  • Don’t use drain cleaner to remove clogs: Many of these products contain harsh chemicals that could damage your plumbing.
  • Don’t try to force silverware or tools down the sink: You could puncture seals, crack your pipes, or force the blockage even further down into the plumbing.
  • Don’t pour boiling water down the drain: It can melt PVC pipes or crack delicate porcelain sink basins.
  • Don’t flush non-biodegradable items down the toilet: Items like paper towels, baby wipes, and feminine products should not be flushed.
  • Don’t pour FOG down the drain: Pouring fats, oils, grease, food scraps, or coffee grounds down the kitchen sink increases the likelihood of developing a clog.

When to Call a Plumber for a Slow Draining Sink

If you’re not sure when to call a plumber for a slow-draining sink, we’ve got the answer. Always contact a qualified professional if more than one drain is slow or clogged at the same time. Gurgling noises or foul odors coming from the drain opening also warrant a call for professional plumbing diagnosis and repair.

How to Prevent Slow Draining Sink Problems

There are some ways you can try to prevent slow draining sink problems in your Wasatch Front area home, including:

  • Know how to prevent frozen drain pipes before the first freeze to keep them from freezing and blocking your drains when cold Utah winter temperatures arrive.
  • Practice routine plumbing maintenance and schedule a whole-home plumbing inspection once a year to find issues early.
  • If you suspect a sewer line blockage is causing the sink to drain slowly, contact the pros at Sunny Home Services for a sewer camera inspection.
  • Use a drain screen in tubs and showers to keep strands of hair, soap scum, and buildup from going down the drain.
  • Educate your household about what not to flush or pour down the drain to prevent clogs.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does it cost to have a plumber clear a sink drain?
The cost to have a plumber clear a sink drain varies based on several factors, including whether it’s just one drain or multiple drains. If you’re dealing with several backups at once, it may cost more, especially if the slow drainage is caused by a sewer line blockage.
Does baking soda and vinegar really work on a slow drain?
Baking soda and vinegar may work on a slow drain if it’s just caused by a very small, shallow clog. This popular DIY drain hack won’t work on most slow drains because it’s not effective enough to actually remove what’s causing the blockage in the first place.
Can frozen pipes cause a clogged drain?
Yes, frozen pipes can cause a clogged drain. As the water in your pipes freezes and expands, it prevents any water from moving through. Freezing temperatures can also cause debris like fats and grease to solidify, leading to a stubborn blockage.
