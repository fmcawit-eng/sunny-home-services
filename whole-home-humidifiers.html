Benefits of Adding a Humidifier to an Existing HVAC System in Wasatch Front Homes

Adding an entire house humidifier to an existing HVAC system provides many benefits to West Jordan and Wasatch Front homes, including:

  • ✓ Helps to hydrate skin: A humidifier in your HVAC system adds moisture to the air that helps hydrate dry throats, nasal passages, chapped lips, and skin.
  • ✓ Stops static: Adding a whole-home humidifier helps to stop static shock that occurs whenever you touch a fabric surface or metal doorknob.
  • ✓ Protects wood: When indoor humidity is at the appropriate level, hardwood floors, antique furniture, and other wood objects are less likely to warp, split, or shrink.
  • ✓ Lowers energy bills: A whole house humidifier for your furnace helps retain heat better than if the air were too dry, saving you money on energy costs by allowing you to keep the temperature setting lower.
  • ✓ Easy to use: Whole house HVAC humidifiers integrate into your existing furnace and work automatically through your air ducts, making them easy to use and operate year-round.
  • ✓ Covers the entire home: Unlike small, portable humidifiers that only treat one room at a time, integrated whole-house humidifiers evenly humidify every room automatically without the need to refill them with water.

Signs You Need a Whole Home Humidifier

Here are some signs that may indicate you need to install a whole house humidifier in West Jordan:

  • You experience frequent nosebleeds or morning congestion, including a stuffy nose, scratchy throat, or dry nasal passages.
  • Your skin feels dry and flaky, even after applying lotion.
  • Every time you walk across the carpet, touch a doorknob, or grab a blanket, you get a static shock.
  • There are noticeable cracks in your hardwood floors, or you see gaps forming in window trim and window frames.
  • Wooden items like chairs and tables are loose or starting to crack.
  • Even when the thermostat temperature is high in your West Jordan home, you still feel cold.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a humidistat, or does it work automatically with my thermostat?
Older standard thermostats only manage temperatures and not humidity, so you may need a separate humidistat to operate your new whole house humidifier. These standalone controls are usually mounted on your wall or somewhere on your ductwork to accurately monitor humidity levels.
What’s the difference between bypass, fan-powered, and steam humidifiers?
A bypass humidifier uses existing furnace heat and duct pressure to humidify the air. Fan-powered systems use a built-in fan to help push moisture, while steam humidifiers boil water independently and provide maximum moisture output.
What humidity level should I set my humidifier to?
In Utah, set your humidifier’s humidity level to between 35% and 45% on normal winter days. During extremely cold periods, set it to between 30% and 35%. You can usually turn your humidifier off during the summer, or at least keep the level below 50% to prevent mold and mildew growth.
