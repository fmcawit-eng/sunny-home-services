What Counts as a Plumbing Emergency

A plumbing emergency is any issue that puts your home, belongings, or safety at risk. These can include:

  • ✓ A burst or leaking pipe
  • ✓ Sewage backing up into a tub, shower, or floor drain
  • ✓ A water heater leaking from the tank
  • ✓ No hot water in the middle of a Wasatch winter
  • ✓ An overflowing toilet you can’t stop

What to Do Before Our Plumbers Arrive

When you’re dealing with a plumbing emergency, start by shutting off your water. You’ll often find your main shut-off valve in the basement, garage, or utility closet near where the line enters the house. Next, cut power at the breaker if the water is near outlets, move what you can out of the water’s path, and take photos for your insurance claim while you wait for our plumbers to arrive.

Draper’s Water & Housing Considerations for Plumbing Service Needs

Draper’s water affects how your plumbing system ages and performs. Wasatch Front water is commonly tested in the very hard range, which causes scale to slowly build up inside water heaters, fixtures, and supply lines every day.

The area’s changing elevation also causes pressure differences in homes throughout the city. Houses that sit at 6,000 feet, around Suncrest, receive much different water pressure than the neighborhoods sitting at the bottom of the bench.

With clay or galvanized supply lines running through older homes near the historic center, it’s common for pipes to corrode, run slow, or fail outright. The rocky, cobble-heavy soil then makes any excavation slower or more complex than other locations in the valley.
