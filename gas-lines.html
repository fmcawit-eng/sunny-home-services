Common Gas Line Issues We Handle

Gas line work on the Wasatch Front usually comes down to a handful of recurring problems driven by the age of the home, the soil, and how appliances have been added over the years.

Utah’s freeze cycles and expansive soils put steady pressure on buried lines, while older interior piping slowly corrodes. Add in newer appliances tied onto undersized runs, and small issues turn into real safety concerns fast.

Sunny Home Services plumbers are frequently called out to address:

  • Gas odors near a line, meter, or appliance connection.
  • Corroded or rusted black steel pipe in older homes.
  • Low pressure that leaves furnaces or ranges underperforming.
  • Failed fittings and unions loosened by ground movement.
  • Improper flex connectors on stoves, dryers, and water heaters.

On the Wasatch Front specifically, we see gas line problems tied to both newer and older homes:

  • In newer homes, added appliances often overload a line that was sized only for the original build.
  • In older homes across the valley, aging steel pipe and buried fittings develop slow leaks that go unnoticed for years.

Because a gas leak is a safety issue and not just a comfort one, we pressure-test and inspect every repair before restoring service, so you never have to wonder whether the line is truly sealed.

Signs You Need Gas Line Repair vs Replacement

Knowing whether to repair a section of gas line on the Wasatch Front, or replace a run entirely, comes down to the age of the pipe, the material, and how many trouble spots the line is showing.

On the Wasatch Front homes, a single loose fitting or damaged section is often an easy repair, while an old steel line that keeps developing leaks in different spots is usually telling you the whole run is at the end of its life. When corrosion is widespread, replacing the line is safer and more cost-effective than chasing one leak after another.

Repair is the right choice when:
  • The problem is isolated to one fitting, union, or short section.
  • The pipe is otherwise sound with no widespread corrosion.
  • A single appliance connector or valve has failed.
  • The line passes a pressure test once the bad spot is fixed.
Replacement becomes more likely when:
  • Leaks keep showing up in different spots along the run.
  • Old black steel pipe is rusted or corroded throughout.
  • The line is badly undersized for your current appliances.
  • The run fails a pressure test even after repairs.
  • You are adding load that the existing line cannot safely carry.

Frequently Asked Questions

What should I do if I smell gas in my Wasatch Front home?
If you smell a strong gas odor, leave the home, avoid switches and flames, and call your gas utility from outside. Once the immediate hazard is handled, Sunny Home Services can locate the source, repair the line, and pressure-test it to confirm the system is sealed before restoring service.
Do you pull permits for gas line work?
Yes. Gas work in Utah must be permitted and inspected. Sunny Home Services handles the permit and inspection process for you, so your new or repaired line is documented, code-compliant, and passes the first time.
Can you run a new gas line for a range, fire pit, or garage heater?
Absolutely. We size and run new gas lines for ranges, dryers, fire tables, patio grills, garage heaters, and pool heaters, making sure the new run and any shared lines can carry the added load without starving your other appliances.
