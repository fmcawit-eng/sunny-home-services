What Is a Sewer & Drain Camera Inspection?

A professional sewer and drain camera inspection is a diagnostic method for identifying problems. These specialized waterproof cameras are attached to a flexible cable, which we feed into sewer and drain lines from access points, such as a cleanout or drain opening. The camera returns a high-resolution live feed, allowing our plumbers to pinpoint the location and nature of the problem without invasive excavation.

What We Look For: Common Issues Found During Inspection

There are a few common issues found during inspections for sewer and drain lines in Wasatch Front homes. We look for problems such as:

  • Tree root intrusion: Silver maple, willow, and other trees with aggressive roots in West Jordan often seek out moisture and can invade pipes, causing significant blockages that require hydrojetting to clear.
  • Sagging or misaligned pipes: Clay soil shifts, erodes, and compacts during freeze-thaw cycles and can cause pipe sections to dip below grade or joints to become misaligned.
  • Aging and deteriorating pipe materials: Many homes in West Jordan have older pipe materials that corrode and crack as they age.
  • Mineral scaling: West Jordan’s hard water supply creates mineral deposits inside pipes, narrowing the flow, increasing the likelihood of clogs, and accelerating corrosion.

Frequently Asked Questions

How often should older homes have their sewer line inspected?
Older homes should have their sewer lines inspected every two to three years, depending on pipe materials, trees in your yard, and general performance. Homes with aging cast iron or clay pipes, mature trees near underground lines, or recurring clogs or slow drains may need inspections more frequently.
Do I need a sewer scope before buying a home?
Many professionals recommend getting a sewer scope inspection near you before buying a home. Many older homes in the Wasatch Front have original cast iron or clay lines that may be deteriorating and near the end of their functional lifespans, and regional factors like clay soils and hard water can accelerate wear.
Can a camera inspection tell me what kind of pipe I have?
Yes, a camera inspection can usually tell you what kind of pipes your home has. The high-resolution feed and lighting from the camera allows technicians to analyze the color, texture, joints, and other details to determine whether the pipes are copper, cast iron, PVC, or other materials.
How should I prepare for a sewer drain camera inspection?
To prepare for a sewer drain camera inspection, locate your main sewer line’s cleanout access point and ensure that it’s easily accessible. The outdoor cleanout is usually located near your home’s foundation wall outside. Look for a covered pipe sticking a few inches out from the dirt.
