Common Ductwork Problems We Repair

Ductwork repair along the Wasatch Front often comes down to a few recurring issues driven by both climate and how homes were originally built.

Utah’s extreme temperature swings put constant stress on duct joints and seals. Other local factors also play a role: ducts routed through unconditioned attics and crawlspaces, decades of settling in older homes, and fine valley dust that infiltrates through gaps in the system.

Sunny Home Services’ technicians are frequently called out to address:

  • Leaking joints and seams that waste conditioned air.
  • Disconnected or crushed duct runs behind walls or in attics.
  • Poorly sealed connections at registers and the air handler.
  • Undersized returns that starve the system of airflow.
  • Rooms that stay too hot in summer or too cold in winter.
  • Old, failing duct tape and deteriorated insulation.

Along the Wasatch Front specifically, we see ductwork problems tied to both newer and older homes:

  • In newer homes, long runs or undersized returns create pressure imbalances that leave some rooms uncomfortable.
  • In older homes across the valley, aging seals and settling open up leaks that grow worse each season.

Across the Salt Lake Valley, duct leakage is one of the biggest hidden causes of high energy bills. Poorly sealed ductwork can lose 20 to 30% of the air your system produces before it ever reaches your rooms.

When to Repair vs Replace Ductwork

Knowing whether to repair your existing ductwork along the Wasatch Front, or replace sections entirely, comes down to the condition of the ducts, how the system is performing, and the design of the original layout.

Along the Wasatch Front homes, ductwork that runs through hot attics and cold crawlspaces tends to wear at the seals faster, while poorly designed layouts often cause comfort problems no amount of sealing will fully fix. If you’re facing both widespread leaks and uneven comfort, replacing problem sections is often more cost-effective than repeated patching.

Repair is the right choice when:
  • Leaks are isolated to a few joints or connections.
  • The overall duct layout and sizing are sound.
  • The ducts are in generally good condition.
  • Comfort issues are limited to one or two rooms.
  • Sealing and minor fixes noticeably improve airflow.
Replacement becomes more likely when:
  • Leaks are widespread across the whole system.
  • Ducts are crushed, corroded, or badly deteriorated.
  • The original design causes chronic comfort problems.
  • Multiple rooms never reach a comfortable temperature.
  • Energy bills stay high despite sealing and repairs.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I know if my ductwork is leaking?
Common signs along the Wasatch Front homes include rooms that never reach a comfortable temperature, higher-than-expected energy bills, weak airflow from certain vents, and excess dust. Our technicians perform a full ductwork inspection to locate leaks and airflow problems, then show you exactly what they find.
Can sealing my ducts really lower my energy bills?
Yes. Poorly sealed ductwork can lose 20 to 30% of the conditioned air your system produces before it reaches your rooms. Because Utah systems run long hours in both summer and winter, sealing those leaks means your system delivers more of the air it makes, which improves comfort and reduces waste.
How long does ductwork repair take?
Many duct repairs are completed in a single visit, often a few hours, depending on the number of leaks and how accessible the ductwork is. Larger repairs or partial replacements take longer and are scheduled with a clear plan, but our technicians work to minimize disruption to your home.
