How Often Should You Replace Air Filters in Wasatch Front Homes?

Book Now All Articles

Swapping out an old, dirt-caked filter for a new and clean air filter is a DIY-friendly job, but it’s sometimes forgotten or completed infrequently. If you’re not sure how often to change your HVAC air filter, it’s a good idea to learn why regular replacements matter and the signs your filter needs replacing. Get a step-by-step overview of how to switch out the filter yourself to enjoy better home comfort and system efficiency between professional maintenance visits from Sunny Home Services.

Key Takeaways

  • How often you should change your air filter depends on conditions in your home and across the Wasatch Front, with most systems requiring replacements every 30 to 90 days.
  • Winter inversions trap smog and cause poor air quality that can quickly clog HVAC filters.
  • Neglecting to change the filter regularly can increase energy bills, cause breakdowns, disrupt your comfort, and cause your system to fail prematurely.
  • Changing your air filter is a straightforward process that only takes a few steps.

HVAC Filter Replacement Schedule for Wasatch Front Homes

The typical HVAC filter replacement schedule for Wasatch Front homes is about every 30 to 60 days. HVAC air filters can last up to 90 days before they become ineffective, but the environment of the Wasatch Front, filter type, and factors specific to your home may require more frequent replacements. During peak periods of HVAC demand in the winter and summer, plan to change the filter every 30 days. In addition to heavy usage, HVAC systems are subjected to severe winter inversions and particulate pollution that doubles each day, as well as summer wildfire smoke and local dust. Most Wasatch Front homes use their HVAC year-round, so filter replacements are still important in the shoulder seasons. In the fall and spring, change the filter every 30 to 60 days. Additionally, filters with high MERV ratings catch more particles but also clog faster than thinner filters. How often air filters are changed also depends on pets, household size, allergies, and other factors. 5-Inch Filter Replacement Schedule If your system has enough space, a 5-inch media filter is standard with a new furnace installation. These thicker filters have more surface area than standard filters, which allows for higher filtration up to a MERV 16 rating. They also last much longer and only need to be replaced every 6 to 12 months. We recommend inspecting your 5-inch filter monthly starting at the 6-month mark to see how much dust and debris it has collected.

Signs Your Air Filter Needs Changing

Knowing the signs that your air filter needs changing allows you to notice and address warnings promptly. Watch for these signs:

  • Weak airflow: Clogged filters restrict airflow from your vents, so it may feel like your HVAC isn’t running.
  • Inconsistent temperatures: Poor airflow and circulation create uneven cooling and heating throughout your home.
  • Odors: Clogged filters may cause musty, stale, or dirty odors when the HVAC system starts a cycle.
  • Dust buildup: Interior surfaces may accumulate dust more quickly if the filter is clogged.
  • Short cycling: Rapid on-and-off cycles are a typical sign of underlying inefficiencies.
  • Constantly running: The system may be unable to reach the thermostat’s target temperature, causing the system to run continuously.

What Happens If You Don’t Change Your Filter?

If you don’t change your filter, your cooling and heating system is more likely to break down and interrupt your home comfort.

Reduced efficiency and higher bills

When dust and debris completely coat the filter, the blower motor must strain to circulate air through the system. Lower air volume through vents extends cooling or heating cycle durations as the system struggles to match temperature demands, resulting in higher energy consumption and utility bills.

Frequent breakdowns and costly repairs

Restricted airflow from clogged filters impacts components throughout the HVAC system, potentially causing problems such as frozen evaporator coils, faulty electrical components, burnt-out motors, or an overheating compressor. These issues have a compounding effect and accelerate system wear, which then increases the risk and frequency of breakdowns. Certain breakdowns of major components, such as failed motors or compressors, can also have high repair costs.

Inconsistent comfort

Restricted airflow and irregular cycles prevent your system from properly circulating and evening out temperatures and humidity. Some areas of your home may have hot or cold spots, and secondary issues resulting from clogged filters can also prevent your system from generating or transferring heat correctly.

Poor indoor air quality

Clogged air filters are unable to trap airborne particles, so contaminants like dust, pet dander, pollen, and mold spores recirculate throughout your HVAC system and impact indoor air quality. With the mountain barriers of the Wasatch Front trapping stagnant air and smog along the valley floor, airborne contaminants are even more likely to enter your HVAC system and trigger or exacerbate allergies, asthma, and other conditions.

Shorter equipment lifespan

Constantly restricted airflow and equipment strain cause premature wear on your HVAC system. Without regular filter replacements and system tune-ups, your heating and cooling units may fail years before a well-maintained system and require a replacement sooner than expected.

Voided manufacturer warranty

Major HVAC manufacturers frequently require you to complete basic upkeep and schedule professional maintenance with Sunny Home Services to keep your warranty valid. Failing to replace air filters regularly may void your warranty and leave you responsible for repair costs in case of a defective part.

How To Change Your Air Filter

To change your air filter, follow these straightforward steps:

  • Step one: Turn the system off at the thermostat or power switch to prevent the HVAC system from starting a cycle while you’re replacing the filter.
  • Step two: Locate the air filter. In most systems, the filter is inside the furnace or next to the furnace/airhandler in a specific air filter cabinet or behind a return grille blower cabinet.
  • Step three: Remove the cover panel, then pull out the filter slowly to inspect it. If it’s caked with dust and debris, or you can’t see light through it, it’s time to replace it. Check the size and MERV rating labeled on the filter frame to confirm the new filter is compatible.
  • Step four: Install the new filter, ensuring that the airflow arrow on the filter’s frame is pointed toward the furnace or blower and/or following the direction of the system’s airflow. The filter should fit snugly into place with no gaps.
  • Step five: Secure the cover panel or grille latch back in place, then turn the power and thermostat back on.

As an optional extra step, make a recurring calendar reminder in your phone to inspect the filter every 30 days. Keep extra air filters stocked to make replacements more convenient.
