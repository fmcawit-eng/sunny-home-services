Common Problems Trenchless Repair Solves

Trenchless sewer repair along the Wasatch Front addresses many of the same failures as traditional repair, but without the open trench.

Utah’s heavy clay soils and mature landscaping crack and invade buried lines. Trenchless methods, like pipe lining and pipe bursting, let us fix those problems from inside the existing line or by pulling new pipe through the old path.

Trenchless vs Traditional Sewer Repair

Knowing whether trenchless repair or traditional open-trench work is right for your Wasatch Front line comes down to the condition of the pipe, its layout, and what a camera inspection reveals underground.

Trenchless is ideal when the existing pipe can host a liner or be burst and replaced along its path. Traditional excavation is the better call when the line has fully collapsed, sags badly, or needs a rerouted or brand-new connection.

Trenchless repair is ideal when:
  • The existing pipe still holds its shape enough to line.
  • The line runs under lawns, trees, driveways, or patios.
  • Damage is from roots, cracks, or corrosion, not full collapse.
  • You want minimal digging and fast restoration.
Traditional excavation is better when:
  • The line has fully collapsed or severely bellied.
  • The pipe path needs to be rerouted.
  • A brand-new line or connection is required.
  • The existing pipe can’t support a liner.
  • Access points for trenchless equipment aren’t feasible.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does trenchless sewer repair along the Wasatch Front cost?
Trenchless repair cost depends on the length and condition of the line and the method used, lining or bursting. It often saves money overall by avoiding the excavation and yard restoration of a full dig. Sunny Home Services provides upfront, flat-rate pricing so you know the cost before any work begins.
Is trenchless repair as durable as replacing the pipe?
Yes. A cured-in-place liner creates a seamless, root-resistant pipe within the old one that can last 50 years or more, and pipe bursting installs brand-new pipe entirely. Both are long-term fixes, not temporary patches.
Can every sewer line be repaired trenchlessly?
Not every one. A fully collapsed or severely bellied line, or a pipe that needs rerouting, may require traditional excavation. That’s why we run a camera inspection first, so we only recommend trenchless when it will fully solve the problem.
