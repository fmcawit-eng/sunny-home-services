How Trenchless Sewer Repair Compares to Traditional Methods

Trenchless sewer repair can reduce yard damage, minimize excavation, and lower restoration needs compared with traditional dig-and-replace work. Traditional replacement calls for a trench that runs the full length of the damaged pipe.

Our plumbing technicians thread through the existing pipe path so your lawn, driveway, and irrigation system can usually stay intact. Because trenchless methods require less excavation, they can also reduce the amount of landscaping, concrete, and other surface restoration needed afterward.

Trenchless Repair Methods We Use in West Jordan, UT

Two common trenchless sewer line repair approaches are cured-in-place pipe lining (CIPP) and pipe bursting. The right option depends on the pipe’s material, condition, alignment, and the extent of the damage.

Cured-in-place pipe (CIPP) lining

A resin-coated liner is inserted into the damaged pipe and cured in place, creating a new pipe within the old one. This no-dig sewer pipe repair method can be appropriate for pipes with cracks, joint problems, or root intrusion when the existing line is suitable for lining.

Pipe bursting

A bursting head fractures the old pipe outward while pulling a new HDPE pipe into its place. Pipe bursting can be used for trenchless sewer replacement when the existing route is suitable, and the surrounding soil and other underground conditions can accommodate the installation.

Signs You Need Trenchless Sewer Line Replacement

Slow drains throughout the house, gurgling toilets, and sewage odors in the yard may call for a new sewer line installation if repairs won’t suffice. When more than one fixture backs up at once, the problem often sits in the main line rather than an isolated drain.

  • ✓ Multiple slow or backed-up drains happening at the same time
  • ✓ Soggy or unusually green patches of lawn above the sewer path
  • ✓ Sewage odor near the cleanout or in the basement
  • ✓ Tree root intrusion, particularly in older sewer lines
  • ✓ Cracks, settling, or unusually soft areas near the suspected sewer-line path

Frequently Asked Questions

Is trenchless sewer repair more expensive than traditional repair?
The upfront cost of trenchless work can be higher than conventional repairs. However, overall project costs may be lower when there is less landscaping or surface restoration afterward.
How long does trenchless repair or replacement take?
Timelines vary with pipe length, depth, access, damage, and repair method. Some trenchless projects can be completed quickly, while more complex work may take longer.
Does trenchless repair work for water lines too?
Trenchless water line replacement can use pipe-bursting and other methods to replace certain underground water lines. Whether it’s appropriate depends on the existing line, site conditions, and local requirements.
Will my yard or driveway be damaged during trenchless repair?
Trenchless methods generally require substantially less excavation than full-length trenching, although the number and size of access points depend on the needed repair and site conditions. This is one of the biggest reasons homeowners choose no-dig sewer repair in West Jordan over a full trench excavation.
How long does a trenchless repair last?
A properly designed and installed CIPP liner or replacement pipe can provide long-term service. The actual lifespan depends on the material, installation, operating conditions, and maintenance.
Do you use camera inspection to confirm trenchless is possible before starting?
A camera inspection is an important diagnostic step for determining whether a trenchless approach is appropriate for a sewer line. Based on the findings, our experienced technicians can walk you through viable options for sewer line repair or replacement.
