Common HVAC Problems We See in Sandy Homes

The HVAC problems we see most in Sandy trace back to the age of the housing stock, the elevation gradient across the city, and the wind coming out of the canyons. Our technicians commonly tackle:

  • ✓ Aging equipment: Many homes throughout Sandy were built between 1970 and 1980, and those that have only had one or two system replacements are running well past their lifespan.
  • ✓ Swamp cooler holdovers: Older homes in Sandy often rely on evaporative coolers that struggle through humid monsoon stretches.
  • ✓ Canyon wind damage: The winds off Little Cottonwood are some of the strongest on the Wasatch Front, and can pack condenser coils with debris, bend fins, and shorten the life of outdoor units.
  • ✓ Uneven heating: Split-level homes in Sandy often contain forced-air systems that weren’t designed to balance airflow across three or four floors.
  • ✓ Cold-weather strain: Homes at higher elevations near Dimple Dell and the canyon mouths have to run their heaters longer and harder to stay comfortable when temperatures drop below freezing.

Signs Your System Needs to Be Replaced

If your heating or cooling system is nearing the end of its lifespan or repairs are starting to add up, it may be time to consider replacement. Common warning signs include:

  • ✓ Your equipment is more than 10 years old.

  • ✓ Your AC still uses R-22 refrigerant.
  • ✓ Rooms never reach the temperature on your thermostat.
  • ✓ Utility costs are rising even though usage is the same.

Sandy’s Unique Climate Considerations for HVAC and Plumbing Services

Sandy’s position on the Wasatch Front creates conditions that force your HVAC and plumbing equipment to work hard all year. Homeowners here have to deal with:

  • ✓ Elevation differences: Houses on the east bench sit significantly higher than those near I-15, which means colder nights, more snow, and longer heating seasons.
  • ✓ Canyon winds: Winds off Little and Big Cottonwood can wear on outdoor units, clog coils with debris, and take out power during major windstorms.
  • ✓ Winter inversions: Trapped valley air concentrates particulates that can make their way inside leaks or gaps in your ductwork.
  • ✓ Hard water: Salt Lake Valley’s hard water causes scale to build inside water heaters, tankless units, fixtures, and supply lines, cutting years off their lifespan.
  • ✓ Mature roots: Decades-old trees around Dimple Dell and older neighborhoods search for moisture in cracked supply lines and separated sewer joints.
