Frequently Asked Questions

Do heat pumps work in cold climates like West Jordan?
Modern cold-climate heat pumps are rated to operate efficiently well below freezing, often down to -13°F, which covers even West Jordan’s coldest winter nights. Many Utah homeowners pair them with a gas furnace backup as a dual-fuel setup for added peace of mind.
Can I install a heat pump if I have an existing furnace?
Yes, many homeowners upgrade to a dual-fuel system that keeps the existing furnace as a backup heat source while a heat pump handles the bulk of the heating and all of the cooling. This setup performs well in Utah’s climate and often qualifies for federal tax credits.
Do I need backup heat in Utah?
A properly sized cold-climate heat pump can handle West Jordan winters on its own, but some homeowners prefer a dual-fuel setup with a gas furnace for the coldest stretches. Sunny’s technicians assess your home and lay out both options with honest recommendations.
What’s the difference between a heat pump and an AC unit?
An air conditioner only moves heat out of your home during summer, while a heat pump moves heat in both directions, out in summer and in during winter. That two-way capability is what makes a heat pump a full year-round heating and cooling solution rather than a one-season appliance.
