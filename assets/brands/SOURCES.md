# Manufacturer logos

Where each file came from, and what it is cleared for.

All six are from Wikimedia Commons and all six are marked **Public domain**
there, on the usual basis that the mark is below the threshold of originality.
That covers copyright. It does not cover trademark, which is the part that
matters here: see the note at the bottom.

`mitsubishi.png` was cropped to the Mitsubishi Electric mark; the source file
carries the Mr. SLIM product line underneath, which is a product range rather
than the brand. Nothing else was altered.

A horizontal Daikin mark exists on Commons but is CC BY-SA 3.0, which would put
an attribution and share-alike obligation on the client's site. The stacked mark
used here is public domain, so it was taken instead.

## Brands shown as text, not logos

**Trane** and **American Standard**: Commons only holds the Trane Technologies
mark, which is the parent company rather than the HVAC brand, and the American
Standard script, which is the plumbing fixtures business rather than American
Standard Heating & Air Conditioning. Both would be the wrong mark.

**Bryant**, **York**, **Fujitsu** and **LG**: no usable file found.

## Before launch

Showing a manufacturer's logo normally signals authorized dealer status, and
most makers restrict their marks to dealers in their own program. The client's
copy says Sunny services these brands and carries parts for them, which is not
the same claim, and nothing in the brief or on their site claims dealer status.

Confirm with the client which brands they are an authorized dealer for, and keep
logos only for those. This is listed in the page log as an open item.
