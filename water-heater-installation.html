Signs You Need a New Water Heater vs Repair

Deciding whether to install a new water heater along the Wasatch Front, or repair the one you have, comes down to the unit’s age, condition, and how hard our water has been on the tank.

Along the Wasatch Front homes, a younger heater with a single failed part is usually worth repairing, while an older tank leaking or dropping rusty water is signaling it’s time to replace. If yours is aging and needing repeated repairs, a new install is often the smarter long-term investment.

Installing a new unit makes sense when:
  • Your heater is 10 to 15+ years old.
  • The tank body is leaking, not just a fitting or valve.
  • Hot water comes out rusty or smells metallic.
  • You keep running out of hot water for your household.
  • Repair costs are climbing on aging equipment.
A repair may still be enough when:
  • The unit is less than 8 to 10 years old.
  • The problem is one isolated part, like a thermostat or valve.
  • There’s no rust in the water and no tank leak.
  • Hot water dropped off suddenly but was fine before.
