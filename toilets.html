Common Toilet Issues We Handle

Toilet problems along the Wasatch Front usually come down to a handful of recurring issues driven by hard water, the age of the fixture, and the parts inside the tank.

Utah’s mineral-heavy water wears out flappers and fill valves, while older wax seals and supply lines give out with age. Add a stubborn clog and a fixture the whole family depends on quickly becomes a problem.

Sunny Home Services plumbers are frequently called out to address:

  • Toilets that run constantly or cycle on their own.
  • Weak or incomplete flushes that require a second flush.
  • Water pooling around the base from a failed wax seal.
  • Recurring clogs in low-flow or older toilets.
  • Loose, rocking toilets from worn bolts or flanges.
  • Cracked tanks, bowls, or failed shutoff valves.

Along the Wasatch Front specifically, we see toilet problems tied to both newer and older homes:

  • In newer homes, early low-flow units sometimes clog often and benefit from an upgraded model.
  • In older homes across the valley, aging seals, valves, and supply lines leak and run more often.

Because a running toilet can quietly waste hundreds of gallons a day, we replace worn tank parts and seals with quality components so the repair actually lasts.

Signs You Need Toilet Repair vs Replacement

Knowing whether to repair a toilet along the Wasatch Front, or replace it entirely, comes down to the age of the fixture, how often it needs attention, and whether the bowl or tank is damaged.

Along the Wasatch Front homes, a running valve or a worn flapper is a quick repair, while a cracked bowl, a fixture that clogs constantly, or one that has been rebuilt several times is usually telling you replacement is the smarter investment.

Repair is the right choice when:
  • The toilet runs due to a worn flapper or fill valve.
  • A leak is coming from the supply line or shutoff valve.
  • The wax seal needs replacing but the fixture is sound.
  • The toilet is fairly new and flushes well otherwise.
Replacement becomes more likely when:
  • The tank or bowl is cracked or leaking through the porcelain.
  • The toilet clogs repeatedly no matter what you do.
  • It has already been rebuilt multiple times.
  • You want a more efficient, better-flushing model.
  • Hard water has corroded the internal parts throughout.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does my toilet keep running in my Wasatch Front home?
A constantly running toilet is usually caused by a worn flapper or a failing fill valve, and hard water in Utah wears these parts out faster. Sunny Home Services replaces the worn components with quality parts so the toilet stops cycling and your water bill drops back down.
Should I repair or replace a toilet that clogs often?
If a newer toilet clogs occasionally, a repair or adjustment often solves it. But an early low-flow model that clogs constantly, or a fixture that has been rebuilt several times, is usually worth replacing with an efficient, better-flushing model that clears the bowl reliably.
How long does a toilet installation take?
A standard toilet replacement is typically completed in one to two hours, including removing the old fixture, setting a fresh wax seal, and testing for leaks. We carry common models and parts so most installs are handled in a single visit.
