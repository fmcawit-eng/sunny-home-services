Common Fixture Issues We Handle

Fixture work on the Wasatch Front usually comes down to a handful of recurring jobs driven by hard water, the age of the plumbing, and home upgrades.

Utah’s mineral-heavy water wears out cartridges and clogs aerators, while old shutoffs and supply lines complicate what should be a simple swap. Whether it is an upgrade or a repair, the details make the difference between a clean job and a callback.

Sunny Home Services plumbers are frequently called out to handle:

  • New faucet, sink, and vanity installations.

On the Wasatch Front specifically, we see fixture problems tied to both newer and older homes:

  • In older homes across the valley, corroded shutoffs and mismatched fittings make replacements more involved.

Because a poorly sealed fixture can leak behind a wall or under a cabinet, we take the time to update worn shutoffs and test every connection before we finish.

Signs You Need Fixture Repair vs Replacement

Knowing whether to repair a fixture on the Wasatch Front, or replace it entirely, comes down to the age of the fixture, the type of problem, and how badly hard water has worn it down.

On the Wasatch Front homes, a worn cartridge or a clogged aerator is a quick repair, while a corroded, constantly dripping fixture or an outdated finish is usually a good candidate for an upgrade that looks better and performs reliably.

Repair is the right choice when:
  • A drip is caused by a worn cartridge or washer.
  • Low flow is due to a scaled aerator or showerhead.
  • The fixture body is in good shape and parts are available.
  • The fixture is fairly new and matches your decor.
Replacement becomes more likely when:
  • The fixture is corroded or leaking from the body.
  • Replacement parts are no longer available.
  • You are updating the look of a kitchen or bathroom.
  • Hard water has worn out the internal components.
  • You want better efficiency, flow, or finish.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can you install a faucet or fixture I bought myself?
Yes. Sunny Home Services regularly installs homeowner-supplied faucets, sinks, and showerheads. We check that it fits your rough-in plumbing, update any worn shutoffs, and test every connection so your new fixture is sealed and leak-free.
Why is the water pressure low at my faucet?
Low pressure at a single fixture is usually caused by hard water scale clogging the aerator or cartridge. We can clean or replace the affected parts, and if the fixture is worn throughout, recommend a replacement that restores full flow.
How long does a fixture installation take?
Most faucet and fixture installs are completed in one to two hours, depending on the condition of the existing shutoffs and connections. Older homes with corroded valves can take a bit longer, and we carry common parts to finish in a single visit whenever possible.
