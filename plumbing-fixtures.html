How Does Hard Water Affect Your Plumbing Fixtures in West Jordan, UT?

West Jordan’s hard water leaves calcium and magnesium deposits on your fixtures that shorten their lifespan and reduce water flow throughout your home. Without a water softener or regular maintenance, you’ll often run into:

  • ✓Scale restricting flow on aerators and showerheads.
  • ✓Grit destroying cartridges and valve seats every time you turn the handle.
  • ✓White crusting around the base of faucets or spotting across chrome and glass.
  • ✓A water heater that works harder as scale settles on the tank floor.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I fix a leaky faucet myself, or do I need a plumber?
If the issue is caused by a worn aerator or toilet flapper, you may be able to fix a leaky faucet yourself. However, if you’re dealing with a seized cartridge, a corroded valve body, or shutoff valves that won’t close, it’s best to hire our licensed plumbers to take care of it.
Why is my faucet dripping all of a sudden?
A sudden faucet drip is often due to a cracked O-ring or worn-out cartridge. Salt Lake Valley’s hard water erodes these parts over time and leaves mineral grit behind, which can cause a fixture to fail all at once. If drips suddenly show up in multiple fixtures, the issue could also be stemming from your home’s water pressure.
Should I repair or replace my plumbing fixture?
If your fixture is under 10 years old and only needs a common part replaced, it’s often worth repairing. Replacement makes more sense when the fixture is corroding, parts are discontinued, or the repair costs are more than that of a new fixture.
How long should plumbing fixtures last in Utah?
Faucets should last between 15 and 20 years, but Salt Lake Valley’s hard water can cut that lifespan short if you don’t have a softener in place. Toilets tend to last between 20 and 30 years, while showerheads and aerators scale the fastest and need replacing every few years.
